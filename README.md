# Ezekiel's Temple

An interactive 3D model of Jerusalem and its temple in three periods, with the temple of Ezekiel's vision (Ezekiel 40–48) laid over it. Solomon's temple and palace (1 Kings 6–7; 2 Chronicles 3–4) are shown as they stood in each period; Ezekiel's temple is built from the measurements in the text and can be switched on as a glowing, colour-coded overlay or as a solid building, so the two can be compared on the same rock.

| Period | The city | The temple |
|---|---|---|
| Solomon's time, c. 950 BC | The City of David and the Ophel, a smaller temple terrace, the Western Hill still farmland, high places on the Mount of Corruption | As built: bronze altar, the Sea on twelve oxen, ten stands with basins, gold-covered doors |
| Ezekiel's time, c. 600 BC | The walled Western Hill and Broad Wall, named gates, Hezekiah's tunnel and the Pool of Siloam, the Silwan tombs | As Ezekiel knew it: Ahaz's great altar with the bronze altar moved north, the Sea on stones, the stands stripped (2 Kgs 16:10–17), the doors stripped of gold (18:16), Jotham's Upper Gate (15:35) |
| After the fall, 573 BC | The year of Ezekiel's vision (40:1): the city burned and broken down, with a Babylonian siege ramp | Burned and roofless, the bronze carried to Babylon (2 Kgs 25:9–17), weeds and jackals in the courts (Lam 5:18) |

## What you can do

- **Take the guided tour**: *Guided tour* follows the man with the measuring reed through Ezekiel 40–48 in the order of the text, in 21 stops. Each stop takes you to the place, sets the hour, draws the measurements the passage gives as gold lines in the scene (in cubits and metres) and shows the passage, its figures and how Solomon's temple compares. At 43:1–5 the glory comes from the east over the Mount of Olives at sunrise, enters by the east gate and fills the house. The river and the city to the south appear when the text reaches them (47:1; 48:30). Step with *Next* and *Back* or the arrow keys, let it play by itself, or press *Explore from here* (Esc) at any stop to look around on your own.
- **Choose a camera** with the *Camera* switch at the top:
  - *Orbit* turns about a point: drag to orbit, right-drag (or two fingers) to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom toward the pointer.
  - *Walk* puts you in the streets in first person, on the ground where you were looking. On a touch screen, put your left thumb down anywhere in the lower left and a stick appears under it (push further to go faster), drag anywhere else to look, tap a thing to read about it, and use the *Jump* and *Run* buttons under your right thumb. With a mouse and keyboard, W A S D or the arrow keys walk, Shift runs, Space jumps; the page takes the pointer so the view follows the mouse (where it cannot, as when the page is embedded, drag to look). You have a body: you slide along walls, climb steps and kerbs, walk on roofs and wall tops, and fall off edges.
  - *Fly* goes anywhere, from a lane in the city to high over the hills, and through walls, with the same looking. The stick or W A S D moves where you look, the *Up* and *Down* buttons or E and Q climb and sink, Shift goes faster, and scrolling sets the speed. You fly faster the higher you are.
  - Esc returns to *Orbit* about whatever you were looking at. A game controller works too: left stick to move, right stick to look.
- **Measure**: click any building, room or object. The panel shows its dimensions in cubits and metres, the Hebrew term, and the verses. Where there is a counterpart in the other temple, one click takes you to it.
- **Choose a period**: *Solomon's time*, *Ezekiel's time* or *After the fall*. The city and the temple change together.
- **Overlay Ezekiel's vision**: *Off* shows the period as it stood. *Glowing* lays Ezekiel's temple over it as light, coloured by part (gold house, orange altar, teal gates, violet chambers, rose western building, blue walls), with a strength slider; parts hidden behind solid buildings show through faintly. *Solid* shows Ezekiel's temple as if built, in place of Solomon's (c. 600 BC and 573 BC). The *Ezekiel vs Solomon* tab has a side-by-side table.
- **Explore the city**: four-room houses on terraces with plank doors under stone lintels, beam ends under the roofs and stone steps up to raised doors; firewood, water jars and querns at the doors; courtyards with hearths, cisterns and rows of storage jars; flax drying on the roofs. Crenellated walls and named gates, with the elders' stone benches inside them and market stalls in the gate squares: grain, fig cakes, oil, pottery, cloth and, at the Fish Gate, dried fish, weighed against stone weights (there were no coins yet). The Stepped Stone Structure, the Gihon Spring towers, pools, gardens and tombs. In 573 BC the same houses stand roofless and blackened over ash, charred beams and broken jars. Click anything, or a label, for its sources.
- **Walk out into the land in spring**: olive groves with gnarled trunks and silver-green leaves, figs, oaks and terebinths, pomegranates and date palms in the King's Garden, all moving in the west wind. Grass and wildflowers grow round your feet (anemones, poppies, crown daisies, mustard, lupins, cyclamen among the rocks). Barley is turning gold and being reaped while the wheat is still green, between dry-stone field walls, with watchtowers, winepresses, olive presses, wells and beehives on the land. In 573 BC the fields have gone to thorns.
- **Look up**: dove flocks wheel over the roofs and settle again, swallows skim the lanes and the temple courts, storks pass north high overhead, and vultures circle over the valleys (more over the ruined city). Dogs scavenge in the lanes, gazelles graze east of the Mount of Olives and bolt if you come close, hyraxes and lizards bask on rocks and walls, and in the afternoon wind dust blows off the roads and chaff off the threshing floors.
- **Look to the horizon**: limestone outcrops and thorny scrub on the hills, the Judean desert falling to the Dead Sea, and the hills of Moab beyond it.
- **Water and reflections**: pools, channels and the bronze Sea ripple and reflect the sky. The pool nearest you mirrors its surroundings. Bronze reflects the courts around it and gold reflects the light of the room it is in.
- **Layers**: the river of Ezek 47 with its 1,000-cubit measuring points, the 50-cubit open land of 45:2, the "500 reeds" reading of 42:16–20 at full scale, and the city of Ezek 48 at full scale.
- **Watch a day go by**: the clock runs by itself. Pause it, drag the hour, or speed it up. The sun crosses the sky and the clouds drift over, their shadows moving across the hills. After dusk the full moon and stars come out, lamps glow in doorways, and fires burn in courtyards, at the gates and on the altar.
- **Meet the people**: they differ in build, age, dress and face, and they are busy. Women grind at saddle querns, bake at clay ovens and weave at upright looms by their doors; potters work at the wheel outside the Potsherd Gate; there are bakers in the bakers' street, elders seated in the gates, scribes, sellers, children playing in the squares and watchmen on the walls. In the fields the barley harvest is under way with reapers, binders and gleaners, oxen draw the threshing sledge, and caravans of camels and donkeys travel the roads. Their work follows the hour: water is drawn at dawn and dusk, the gates empty at sunset, families sit on the roofs in the evening. Priests in linen and worshippers fill the temple courts. In 573 BC almost nobody is left.

Links: `#950`, `#600` and `#573` open a period; add `-glow` or `-solid` for the vision, for example `#600-glow` or `#573-solid`. Older links (`#ezekiel`, `#solomon-600`, `#overlay`) still work. `?tour=5` opens the guided tour at stop 5.

## Run it

It is a static page with no build step. Serve the folder with any web server and open it in a browser with WebGL:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8000
# then open http://localhost:8000
```

Opening `index.html` straight from disk will not work, because browsers block ES modules on `file://`. Three.js 0.160.0 and the fonts load from public CDNs.

Graphics are chosen for the device: computers, tablets and recent phones start on full quality at the screen's own resolution (textured stone with relief, two shadow maps, ambient occlusion, bloom, volumetric clouds, mirror reflections), and the page then trades resolution and effects for speed on the frame rate it measures. Old phones and software renderers start on a light mode. The *Graphics* switch at the bottom of the Views panel overrides this (Auto, High, Low) and lists anything that went wrong on your device. In the address, `?quality=low` or `?quality=high` and `fx=on` or `fx=off` do the same; `clock=stop` starts with the clock paused. On a tall phone screen the view widens so that a building still fits.

## The measurements

[`docs/MEASUREMENTS.md`](docs/MEASUREMENTS.md) lists every measurement in Ezekiel 40–48 with its verse, the matching figures for Solomon's temple, the cross-checks (the gate parts add up to 50 cubits, the house to 100, the precinct to 500), and every assumption the model makes where the text is silent.

[`docs/HISTORY.md`](docs/HISTORY.md) covers the city and countryside: what each period shows, and for every feature whether it is attested, probable or conjectural, with sources.

[`docs/MODULES.md`](docs/MODULES.md) describes how the detail modules (props, trades, fauna, flora) plug into the page.

Key choices:

| Question | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Cubit | Long cubit, 52.5 cm | "A cubit and a handbreadth" (40:5) |
| 500 reeds or cubits? (42:16–20) | Cubits | The inner measurements sum to 500 cubits; LXX reads cubits |
| Where on the map? | Most Holy Place over the rock on the Temple Mount | Traditional site of both temples |
| Heights Ezekiel does not give | House 30 cubits, gates 20 | Solomon's height; LXX of 40:14 |
