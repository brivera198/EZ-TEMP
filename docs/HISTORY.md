# Jerusalem in the model: periods, evidence and assumptions

The city and countryside around the temple change with the period you choose. This file lists what is shown in each period and how firm the evidence is.

Confidence labels:

- **Attested**: named or described in the Bible and/or found in excavation.
- **Probable**: the feature existed, but its exact form or position is reconstructed.
- **Conjectural**: a reasoned guess where the evidence is thin or disputed.

## The three periods

| Period | Shown with | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **c. 950 BC**: Solomon's Jerusalem | Solomon's temple | City of David and Ophel only. Western Hill is farmland. Smaller temple terrace. High places on the Mount of Corruption. No Siloam pool or tunnel. |
| **c. 600 BC**: before the fall | Ezekiel's or Solomon's temple | City of David plus the walled Western Hill (Mishneh), Broad Wall, named gates, Hezekiah's tunnel and the Pool of Siloam, Silwan tombs, the 500-cubit Temple Mount square. |
| **573 BC**: the year of the vision | Ezekiel's temple | Same city burned and broken down: roofless houses, breached walls, a siege ramp, trees cut near the walls, no people in the streets. |

Ezekiel's vision is dated "in the fourteenth year after the city was struck down" (Ezek 40:1), which is 573 BC. Showing his temple above the ruined city is the most literal setting. The c. 600 BC view shows the city he knew before he was taken to Babylon in 597 BC.

## City size and walls

| Feature | Confidence | Notes and sources |
|---|---|---|
| 10th-century city on the City of David ridge | Attested (extent debated) | 1 Kgs 3:1; 9:15. How large and how fortified Jerusalem was in the 10th century is one of the most argued questions in biblical archaeology (compare E. Mazar with I. Finkelstein). The model shows a modest walled town of about 12–15 ha. |
| Western Hill enclosed by about 700 BC | Attested | The Broad Wall, excavated by N. Avigad; Isa 22:10; 2 Chr 32:5. About 60 ha within the walls. |
| Course of the late Iron Age wall | Probable | Fixed points: Broad Wall, the "Israelite Tower", City of David east wall. Elsewhere it follows the natural slopes. |
| Gate names | Attested; locations conjectural | Corner Gate and Gate of Ephraim (2 Kgs 14:13, with the 400-cubit distance used in the model), Fish Gate (Zeph 1:10), Valley Gate (2 Chr 26:9), Horse Gate (Jer 31:40), Potsherd Gate (Jer 19:2). Water, Fountain and Dung Gates are named in Nehemiah (c. 445 BC). |
| Towers, crenellations, shields on the battlements | Probable | Assyrian reliefs of the siege of Lachish (701 BC, British Museum) show a Judahite city with these features; Ezek 27:11. |
| Population | Conjectural | Estimates for c. 600 BC range from about 8,000 to 25,000; for the 10th century, a few thousand at most. |

## Houses and streets

| Feature | Confidence | Notes and sources |
|---|---|---|
| Four-room (pillared) houses | Attested | The standard Israelite and Judahite house plan; examples on the east slope of the City of David (House of Ahiel). |
| Flat roofs with parapets, upper rooms | Attested | Deut 22:8; 2 Kgs 4:10; Josh 2:6. |
| Houses on terraces following the slope | Probable | Excavated on the City of David's east slope. The model generates rows along the contours with lanes between them. |
| Main streets and squares at the gates | Probable | "The square at the gate" (2 Chr 32:6; Neh 8:1). Street lines in the model are schematic. |
| Burned houses of 586 BC | Attested | Burnt Room, House of the Bullae (51 seal impressions, one naming Gemariah son of Shaphan, Jer 36:10). |

## Temple Mount

| Feature | Confidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Solomon's terrace (c. 950 BC) | Conjectural | No remains identified. Size chosen to hold the courts. |
| 500-cubit square (c. 600 BC and Ezekiel) | Probable; date debated | Mishnah, Middot 2:1. L. Ritmeyer dates the square to Hezekiah (c. 700 BC). |

## People and animals

Figures give scale and show how spaces were used: priests in linen in the inner courts (Ezek 44:17–19), the people in the outer courts, Levite singers east of Solomon's altar (2 Chr 5:12), elders at the city gates (Prov 31:23), farmers, travellers with donkeys, and mixed flocks of fat-tailed sheep and black goats (Lev 3:9; Song 4:1). In Ezekiel's 573 BC view the only figures in the temple are Ezekiel and the man with the measuring reed (Ezek 40:3).

## Terrain

The terrain is a schematic model built from published elevations (Temple Mount summit 743 m, Mount of Olives 818 m, Gihon Spring about 636 m, En-rogel about 600 m) and approximate valley courses. It is not survey data.
