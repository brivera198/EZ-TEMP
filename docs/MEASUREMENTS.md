# Ezekiel 40–48: the measurements

This is the source record behind the 3D model. Every number below comes from the verse cited. Where the model has to assume something the text does not give, the assumption is listed in the last section.

Verse numbers follow English Bibles. "MT" is the Hebrew (Masoretic) text. "LXX" is the Greek Septuagint, which sometimes keeps a better reading in these chapters.

## 1. Units

| Unit | Definition | Metric (model) | Text |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long cubit | "A cubit and a handbreadth" = 7 handbreadths | 52.5 cm | 40:5; 43:13 |
| Reed | 6 long cubits | 3.15 m | 40:5 |
| Span | ½ cubit | 26 cm | 43:13 |
| Handbreadth | 1/7 long cubit | 7.5 cm | 40:5, 43 |

Solomon's temple was built in "cubits of the first measure" (2 Chr 3:3), usually taken to be this same older, longer cubit. The model uses 52.5 cm for both temples, so the comparison is like for like.

Weights and dry/liquid measures (45:10–14): ephah = bath = 1/10 homer; shekel = 20 gerahs; mina = 20 + 25 + 15 = 60 shekels; 10 baths = 1 homer = 1 cor.

## 2. Outer wall and the whole precinct

| Item | Measurement | Text |
|---|---|---|
| Outer wall | 1 reed thick, 1 reed high | 40:5 |
| Precinct | 500 × 500 on each side, "to separate the holy from the common" | 42:15–20 |
| Open land round the sanctuary | 50 cubits on every side | 45:2 |
| Sanctuary square | 500 × 500 | 45:2 |

**500 reeds or 500 cubits?** The Hebrew text of 42:16–19 says "reeds" (1,575 m a side). The Greek says "cubits" (262.5 m). The inner measurements add up to 500 cubits exactly, east to west:

> outer gate 50 + outer court 100 + inner gate 50 + inner court 100 + house 100 + separate place and western building 100 = **500**

and north to south:

> gate 50 + court 100 + gate 50 + inner court 100 + gate 50 + court 100 + gate 50 = **500**

So the model uses 500 cubits. The "500 reeds" layer shows the other reading to scale: it would cross the Kidron Valley and take in most of the city.

## 3. The gates (outer: 40:6–27; inner: 40:28–37)

All six gates have the same plan. East, north and south only. There is no west gate.

| Steps up to an inner gate | 8 | 40:31, 34, 37 |
| Outer threshold | 1 reed deep | 40:6 |

| Space between rooms | 5 cubits | 40:7 |
| Inner threshold by the vestibule | 1 reed | 40:7 |
| Vestibule | 8 cubits | 40:9 |
| Jambs of the vestibule | 2 cubits | 40:9 |
| Width of the entrance | 10 cubits | 40:11 |
| Width of the passage ("length of the gate") | 13 cubits | 40:11 |
| Barrier in front of the rooms | 1 cubit | 40:12 |
| Width of the gate, back wall to back wall | 25 cubits | 40:13 |
| Jambs | "60 cubits" (MT; obscure) | 40:14 |
| Length, entrance to vestibule | 50 cubits | 40:15 |
| Windows | narrow, all round | 40:16 |
| Decoration | palm trees on the jambs | 40:16 |
| Gate to opposite gate | 100 cubits | 40:19, 23, 27 |

Check: threshold 6 + room 6 + space 5 + room 6 + space 5 + room 6 + threshold 6 + vestibule 8 + jambs 2 = **50**. Rooms 6 + passage 13 + rooms 6 = **25**.

The inner gates face the other way: their vestibules open onto the outer court (40:31, 34, 37).

**East gate.** The glory of the LORD comes in by it (43:1–5). After that it stays shut, and only the prince may sit in it to eat bread before the LORD (44:1–3). The east gate of the inner court opens on Sabbaths and new moons (46:1–3).

## 9. The river (47:1–12)

Water comes out from under the threshold of the house, on the south side, south of the altar. It flows east and out under the south side of the outer east gate (47:1–2). The guide measures:

| Distance from the temple | Depth | Text |
|---|---|---|
| 1,000 cubits (525 m) | to the ankles | 47:3 |
| 2,000 cubits | to the knees | 47:4 |
| 3,000 cubits | to the waist | 47:4 |
| 4,000 cubits (2.1 km) | a river too deep to cross; only swimming | 47:5 |

It goes down to the Arabah and the Dead Sea, which becomes fresh. Fishermen stand from En-gedi to En-eglaim. Fruit trees on both banks bear every month, and their leaves heal (47:8–12).

The unit in chapters 45 and 48 is not stated. In cubits the city is 2.36 km square. Ezekiel puts it in its own strip south of the priests' land, not around the temple. With the priests' strip in the middle, the city's centre lies 7,500 cubits (3.9 km) south of the sanctuary. The "Ezekiel 48 city" layer shows that.

## 12. What the model assumes

The text does not give these, so the model fills them in. Each is shown on its info card.

| Assumption | Value used | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Length of a cubit | 52.5 cm | Common value for the royal cubit (Egyptian royal cubit ≈ 52.4 cm) |
| Placement | Most Holy Place over the rock (es-Sakhra) | Traditional site of both temples |
| Unit in 42:16–20 | cubits | Matches the internal sums; follows LXX |
| Step height | ½ cubit | Not given |
| Porch depth | 12 (LXX) | MT 11 does not make the house total 100 |
| Height of the house | 30 cubits | Not given by Ezekiel; Solomon's height |
| Height of the gates | 20 cubits | 40:14 is obscure; LXX reads 20 |
| Size of the 30 chambers | 30 × 20 | Not given |
| Height of the western building | 20 cubits | Not given |
| Solomon's courts | inner 200 × 100; great court = the 500-cubit square | Not given; tabernacle court doubled; Mishnah, Middot 2:1 |
| Solomon's wall thickness | 6 cubits | Not given; as Ezekiel |
| Platform levels of Solomon's house | as Ezekiel's | Not given |
| Palace layout | Forest House, Hall of Pillars, Throne Hall, palace, south of the temple | Order of 1 Kgs 7:1–12; sizes not given except where stated |
| Jerusalem terrain | approximate | Valleys and heights from published elevations, not a survey |
| City wall | late Iron Age II line | Simplified from excavated segments (Broad Wall, City of David) |
